Introduction

May 2025

Major events

May was the month AI became statecraft. The Commerce Department moved to rescind the Biden-era rule that would have rationed AI chips by country, and within a week President Trump’s Gulf trip produced deals for Nvidia and AMD chips in Saudi Arabia and a 5-gigawatt AI campus in Abu Dhabi. Nvidia’s Jensen Huang called US export controls on China “a failure.” A new pope, Leo XIV, explained his name by pointing to the first industrial revolution and the age of AI, while 40 state attorneys general fought a proposed 10-year ban on state AI laws.

The releases kept coming. OpenAI kept its nonprofit in control of a restructured company, was reported to be buying Windsurf and launched OpenAI for Countries. Google DeepMind’s AlphaEvolve used Gemini and automated tests to search for better algorithms, Google’s I/O brought AI Mode to US search and Veo 3 video with sound, and Anthropic released Claude Opus 4 and Sonnet 4. Meta launched a standalone assistant app and said Llama had been downloaded 1.2 billion times.

Patterns and trends

The month turned on how far to trust what developers said about their own systems. A study questioned how the Chatbot Arena leaderboard ranked models. OpenAI rolled back a GPT-4o update that had become excessively agreeable and explained what its testing had missed; xAI blamed an unauthorized prompt change for Grok’s off-topic claims about South Africa. In each case the evidence came largely from the companies themselves, and the question was how outsiders could check it.

Long-term threads

Chip diplomacy became a year-long story: in August Nvidia and AMD agreed to share revenue from China sales with Washington, and in November Microsoft received licenses to ship advanced chips to the UAE while China was reported to bar foreign chips from state-funded data centers. The fight over state AI laws continued into June, and California passed its frontier-AI disclosure law in September. AI Mode gained Gemini 2.5 Pro and Deep Search in July, reached more than 180 more countries in August and added five languages in September. AlphaEvolve’s automated search anticipated the AI-scientist systems the panel examined in October. And Windsurf, reported here as an OpenAI acquisition, ended up in July with its leaders joining Google DeepMind.

P-2025-W18-01

A study questions Chatbot Arena’s ranking practices

April 29

The Leaderboard Illusion examined private model testing, selective publication of results and unequal access to evaluation data on Chatbot Arena. Its authors argued that these practices could advantage some providers and make scores less representative of general model quality.1

The study turned attention from the rankings themselves to the rules that produced them.

Meta launches a standalone AI app

April 29

Meta launched a standalone app for its AI assistant, built with Llama 4. The first version supported voice and text conversations and included a Discover feed where users could choose to share prompts and responses.2

The app gave the assistant a dedicated interface alongside its presence in Meta’s social and messaging services, with personalization and optional sharing central to the design.

Meta says Llama has been downloaded 1.2 billion times

April 29

At LlamaCon, Meta’s first developer conference for its open-weight models, chief product officer Chris Cox said Llama models had been downloaded 1.2 billion times, up from 1 billion in mid-March, and that the Meta AI assistant had about a billion users.3

The figures, reported by Meta, measured the spread of its open-weight models.

P-2025-W18-04

OpenAI rolls back an overly agreeable GPT-4o update

April 29; explanation May 2

OpenAI withdrew a GPT-4o update after it produced excessively agreeable responses in ChatGPT. The company said it had placed too much weight on short-term user feedback. A May 2 account described shortcomings in its testing and plans to improve evaluation before deployment.4,5

The incident exposed a difference between pleasing a user and helping one: feedback that rewards an agreeable answer can miss whether that answer is sound.

Waymo and Toyota explore autonomous-driving collaboration

April 29

Waymo and Toyota announced a preliminary agreement to explore collaboration on autonomous driving. Their proposed work included a new vehicle platform and possible uses of Waymo’s technology in personally owned vehicles, with Woven by Toyota also involved.6

The proposal connected an autonomous-driving developer with a large vehicle manufacturer; its scope remained under discussion.

Microsoft expands the Phi family with reasoning models

April 30

Microsoft introduced Phi-4-reasoning, Phi-4-reasoning-plus and Phi-4-mini-reasoning. The models extended its work on compact language models to tasks requiring additional reasoning, including mathematics, science and coding.7

The releases pursued reasoning capability within smaller models suited to particular tasks and resource budgets.

Amazon releases Nova Premier

April 30

Amazon announced general availability of Nova Premier through Bedrock. The multimodal model was designed for complex inputs and workflows, including long documents and video. It could also serve as a teacher model for producing smaller, task-specific models through distillation.8

The release linked a larger model to the development of smaller ones adapted to a narrower task and operating budget.

An AI-generated image of Trump as pope draws criticism

May 2

President Trump posted an apparently AI-generated image of himself dressed as the pope on Truth Social, and the White House reposted it on X, days before the conclave to elect Pope Francis’s successor. The New York State Catholic Conference and other Catholic leaders criticized the post.9

The episode showed generated images entering political communication at the highest level.

OpenAI keeps nonprofit control in its restructuring plan

May 5

OpenAI announced a revised plan under which its nonprofit would retain control. Its for-profit subsidiary would become a public benefit corporation, with the nonprofit also holding a substantial ownership stake.10

The announcement set out the intended relationship between governance, investment and mission; the restructuring itself was still to be completed.

OpenAI is reported to agree to buy Windsurf

Reported May 6

Bloomberg News reported that OpenAI had agreed to buy Windsurf, the AI coding tool formerly known as Codeium, for about $3 billion. The deal had not closed; OpenAI declined to comment.11

The deal never closed: in July, Google hired Windsurf’s leaders and licensed its technology instead.12

OpenAI proposes national AI partnerships

May 7

OpenAI introduced OpenAI for Countries, an initiative offering partnerships on AI infrastructure, locally adapted services and startup ecosystems. The company connected the program to its Stargate infrastructure effort and described it as supporting democratic approaches to AI.14

The proposal extended the company’s ambitions from individual products to national infrastructure, with each partnership to be negotiated separately.

P-2025-W19-05

The administration moves to rescind the AI diffusion rule

Plan reported May 7; rescission and non-enforcement announced May 13

The Commerce Department said it would rescind the Biden-era AI diffusion rule, in effect since January 13, whose main compliance requirements were due to begin on May 15. The rule would have limited countries’ access to advanced AI chips in tiers. A spokesperson called the rule “overly complex, overly bureaucratic” and promised “a much simpler rule.”15 On May 13 the department announced the rescission and told officials not to enforce the rule; a regulation formalizing the rescission and a replacement rule were still to come. It also warned companies against using Huawei’s Ascend chips.16

Export policy shifted from a global framework toward bilateral deals; Reuters reported that country-by-country agreements could replace the tiers.

Amazon introduces touch-sensitive warehouse robotics

May 7

Amazon introduced Vulcan, a robotic system that uses force and touch sensing to help pick and stow items in fulfillment centers. The company described how sensing contact allows the system to adjust its movements around objects.17,18

Vulcan showed how tactile sensing could support manipulation in a structured warehouse environment, where physical work depends on feedback from the world.

FDA sets a timetable for internal AI deployment

May 8

The US Food and Drug Administration announced plans to expand generative AI across its centers by June 30, following an internal scientific-review pilot. The intended tools would assist staff with repetitive review tasks on a secure platform.19

The timetable put an AI rollout at the center of a regulator’s internal work; the agency launched its tool, Elsa, on June 2.20

Pope Leo XIV links his name to the age of AI

May 10

In his first address to the College of Cardinals, Pope Leo XIV said he chose his name after Leo XIII, whose encyclical “addressed the social question in the context of the first great industrial revolution.” The Church, he said, now offers its social teaching “in response to another industrial revolution and the developments of artificial intelligence.”21

The new pope placed AI among the central questions of his pontificate.

P-2025-W19-09

OpenAI and Microsoft are reported to renegotiate their partnership

Reported May 11

The Financial Times reported that OpenAI and Microsoft were renegotiating their partnership as OpenAI prepared to turn its for-profit arm into a public benefit corporation, a change Microsoft had to approve. The talks concerned the size of Microsoft’s stake, after investments of more than $13 billion, and its access to OpenAI’s technology after the current contract ends in 2030.22

The talks tied the governance plan announced earlier in the week to the commercial terms of OpenAI’s most important partnership.

P-2025-W20-01

US AI deals are announced across the Gulf

May 13–15

During President Trump’s Gulf visit, Nvidia said it would sell more than 18,000 of its latest AI chips to Humain, a company owned by Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund, and AMD announced its own supply deal with Humain.23 In Abu Dhabi, the White House announced a 5-gigawatt AI campus to be built by G42, which Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick said came with security guarantees against diversion of US technology.24

The deals made the Gulf a major destination for American AI chips.

AlphaEvolve searches for better algorithms

May 14

Google DeepMind introduced AlphaEvolve, a system combining Gemini models with automated evaluators and evolutionary search. It generated candidate programs, scored them and used promising results to guide further proposals. DeepMind reported applications in mathematics and its own computing infrastructure.25

The approach suited problems where candidate solutions could be tested automatically.

P-2025-W20-03

xAI responds to off-topic political claims from Grok

May 14–16

After Grok inserted claims about “white genocide” in South Africa into unrelated conversations, xAI attributed the behavior to an unauthorized change to its system prompt. Reuters reported that the company promised to publish its system prompts and add monitoring.26

The episode made control over a chatbot’s instructions a public issue; the account of its cause came from xAI.

State attorneys general oppose a proposed AI-law moratorium

May 16

A coalition of 40 state attorneys general wrote to congressional leaders opposing a proposed 10-year ban on state enforcement of laws addressing AI and automated decision-making. The proposal appeared in committee changes to a budget reconciliation bill.27

The dispute concerned who could act when AI caused harm; the Senate removed the moratorium on July 1.28

Google expands AI Mode in Search

May 20

Google began rolling out AI Mode in the United States without requiring Labs enrollment. The search interface supported follow-up questions and linked answers, using multiple searches across subtopics to assemble a response.29

The rollout made conversational exploration a more prominent part of search.

Veo 3 generates video with sound

May 20

Google introduced Veo 3, a video-generation model that could produce sound along with images, including ambient noise and dialogue. At launch, US Google AI Ultra subscribers could access it through Gemini and Flow.30

Generating sound and moving images together removed a separate step in producing a clip.

Flow brings generative models into a filmmaking workspace

May 20

Google introduced Flow, a filmmaking tool combining Veo, Imagen and Gemini. It was designed to help creators develop clips and scenes while managing visual elements within a shared workspace.31

Flow addressed developing a sequence rather than one isolated clip.

Huang calls US chip export controls a failure

May 21

Speaking at Computex in Taipei, Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang said, “All in all, the export control was a failure,” adding that Nvidia’s share of the Chinese market had fallen to 50 percent from 95 percent at the start of the Biden administration.32

The remarks put the industry’s leading chipmaker openly at odds with the policy of restricting AI chips to China.

Anthropic releases Claude Opus 4 and Sonnet 4

May 22

Anthropic released Claude Opus 4 and Claude Sonnet 4. Both offered rapid responses and an extended-thinking mode, with availability through Claude, Anthropic’s API, Amazon Bedrock and Google Cloud’s Vertex AI. Anthropic emphasized coding and agent workflows in its evaluations.33

The release continued the integration of reasoning modes with software tools, measured on the developer’s chosen benchmarks.
