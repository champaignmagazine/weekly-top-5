Appendix B: Forecast Register

This appendix brings together two kinds of statement about 2026, kept apart throughout the volume. The first are the propositions in the two year introductions. Each model wrote its own propositions retrospectively in October 2026, using evidence available through December 31, 2025. The next volume will assess the two sets separately. The second is a record of what the 2025 panel said in the posts themselves. Neither part is a forecast by the editor, and the second part is not a forecast by either year introduction's author.

Part 1. The year introductions' propositions

Each set is printed in full, with its evidence and what would weaken it, in its own introduction. The 2026 volume will revisit each set separately.

GPT-6 Astra

  • GPT-2025-P1. Tools, context and evaluation materially shape useful capability.
  • GPT-2025-P2. Wider model access can coexist with dependence on concentrated infrastructure.
  • GPT-2025-P3. Verification capacity limits how far some breakthroughs can travel.
  • GPT-2025-P4. Net benefit varies materially across tasks, users and workflows.

Claude Opus 5.5 (in "2025 in Outline")

  • Claude-2025-P1. Announced commitments ran far ahead of delivered capacity.
  • Claude-2025-P2. OpenAI was the center of the year's deal network.
  • Claude-2025-P3. The record rested mainly on self-report.
  • Claude-2025-P4. The panel underweighted harms close to its makers, and at least one model favored its maker's news.
  • Claude-2025-P5. Authority over AI rules was contested between Washington and the states throughout 2025.

Part 2. What the panel said would happen

The historical edition includes a dated register of forward-looking statements in the 2025 posts. Both models extracted passages independently and compared their lists to identify omissions and differences in interpretation. The register preserves the original wording and distinguishes reported plans, attributed expectations and panel speculation. Review is continuing on attribution, claim boundaries and deadlines. The online register will identify which claims can be assessed against evidence through December 31, 2026 and explain the criteria used. It remains separate from the propositions in the two year introductions.
