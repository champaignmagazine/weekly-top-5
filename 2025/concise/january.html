Introduction

January 2025

Major events

January was the month DeepSeek changed the conversation. On January 20 the Chinese laboratory released R1, a reasoning model competitive with OpenAI’s o1 on its reported benchmarks, with weights anyone could download. A week later Nvidia lost roughly $590 billion in market value in a single day as investors reassessed what AI might cost. Within days Microsoft offered R1 on its cloud, while Italy restricted DeepSeek’s use of personal data and NASA blocked it on agency systems.

The infrastructure plans of the month were the largest yet. Microsoft expected to spend about $80 billion on data centers in its fiscal year and Meta $60 billion to $65 billion in 2025; OpenAI, SoftBank, Oracle and MGX announced Stargate, intending to invest $500 billion in US AI infrastructure. Washington changed hands in the middle of it. The outgoing administration issued a rule controlling exports of AI chips and model weights and an order supporting data centers on federal land; the incoming one ordered a new AI action plan within 180 days.

Patterns and trends

Capability and access moved together. Strong models arrived as downloadable weights (R1, Microsoft’s Phi-4, MiniMax-01, Janus-Pro, Ai2’s Tülu 3 405B), and governments answered with decisions about who could use them and where chips could go. Assistants also began to act on their own: ChatGPT gained scheduled tasks, OpenAI’s Operator previewed an agent that used a browser, deep research produced cited reports, and o3-mini brought reasoning to ChatGPT’s free users. Sam Altman suggested that AI agents could join the workforce in 2025, and Mark Zuckerberg that an AI would “probably” be able to do a mid-level engineer’s work that year.

Long-term threads

Several of the year’s longest stories begin here. Stargate grew through the year: to Norway at the end of July, five more US sites in September and Korean memory suppliers in October. The 180-day order produced America’s AI Action Plan in July, and the chip-export rule issued in January was rescinded in May. DeepSeek kept releasing open weights, with V3-0324 in March, V3.1 in August and V3.2-Exp in October, and its R1 research appeared in Nature in September. Operator’s browser agent was folded into ChatGPT agent in July. Nvidia’s January fall proved temporary: by June it was again the world’s most valuable company, and in October it became the first worth $5 trillion.

P-2025-W01-01

OpenAI misses its own date for a creator opt-out tool

Reported January 1

OpenAI had said in May 2024 that Media Manager, a tool to let creators specify how their work could be used in AI training, would be in place “by 2025.” As the year began, it had not appeared, and the company had given no public update since a spokesperson said in August that it was still in development.1

The tool was meant to answer creators’ objections at a time when OpenAI faced several copyright suits over its training data.

Russia seeks closer AI cooperation with China

January 1: instructions reported

Instructions published on the Kremlin’s website called on Russia’s government and Sberbank to develop AI cooperation with China, Reuters reported. The direction concerned joint research and development.2

The proposal placed international cooperation alongside domestic investment as a route to developing AI capacity.

Microsoft plans $80 billion for AI data centers

January 3

Microsoft said it expected to invest approximately $80 billion during its 2025 fiscal year in data centers for training AI models and deploying AI and cloud applications.3

The announcement, a spending plan for the year, placed computing capacity at the center of the company’s AI strategy.

Meta removes AI character profiles

January 3

Meta removed company-created AI character accounts after renewed attention and criticism from users. CNN reported the removals and the difficulties users encountered when trying to block the accounts.4

The episode exposed a practical problem for synthetic identities on social platforms: users needed clear information and working controls over their interactions.

Altman sets out expectations for AI agents

January 5, Pacific time

In “Reflections,” Sam Altman expressed confidence that OpenAI knew how to build AGI as the company had traditionally understood it. He also suggested that AI agents could enter the workforce during 2025.5,6

His forecast made the performance of agents in actual work the test of the company’s ambitions for the year.

P-2025-W02-01

Nvidia brings AI hardware and world models to CES

January 6

At CES, Nvidia introduced the Cosmos platform for developing physical AI, alongside GeForce RTX 50-series graphics processors and Project DIGITS, a desktop AI system. Cosmos combined world foundation models with tools intended to support robotics and autonomous-vehicle development.7

The announcements connected model development to simulation, consumer hardware and local computing beyond a hosted chatbot.

Anthropic is reported to seek $2 billion at a $60 billion valuation

Reported January 7

The Wall Street Journal reported that Anthropic was in talks to raise $2 billion at a $60 billion valuation, in a round led by Lightspeed Venture Partners.8

The talks put a price on investors’ expectations for one of OpenAI’s main rivals at the start of the year.

Microsoft releases Phi-4 weights

January 8

Microsoft made the official Phi-4 model weights available on Hugging Face under the MIT license. The 14-billion-parameter text model had been introduced in December; January’s development was the public release of its weights.9,10

Downloadable weights let developers run and adapt the model on their own computing resources.

Grok reaches US users through a standalone app

January 9

Grok became available as a standalone iOS app in the United States. The release gave users a separate mobile interface for the assistant, which had previously been associated with the X platform.11

Distribution was part of the competition among assistants: a dedicated app could reach users through a different entry point.

P-2025-W03-01

US rule sets new controls on AI diffusion

January 13; published January 15

The US government issued an interim final rule controlling exports of advanced computing chips and certain AI model weights. The rule took effect on January 13 and set May 15 as the compliance date for most changes.15

The framework treated access to computing and model weights as matters of national security.

ChatGPT adds scheduled tasks

January 14

OpenAI began a beta rollout of Tasks for ChatGPT Plus, Pro and Team users. Users could ask ChatGPT to carry out a request later, either once or on a recurring schedule.17

The feature extended the assistant beyond an immediate question-and-answer session; scheduling offered a limited, concrete form of delegation.

MiniMax releases long-context models

January 15

P-2025-W03-06

Apple pauses AI summaries of news alerts

January 16

Apple paused Apple Intelligence notification summaries for news and entertainment apps in developer previews of its next software updates, after complaints that summaries had misrepresented news reports, including a BBC complaint about a false alert. Summaries would be shown in italics and marked as a beta feature that might contain errors.20

The pause showed the risk of generated summaries appearing under a news organization’s name.

DeepSeek releases R1 reasoning models

January 20

DeepSeek released R1 and six smaller distilled models, making their weights available for download. The company reported results competitive with OpenAI’s o1 on specified reasoning benchmarks.21

The release widened access to reasoning models and gave researchers systems they could inspect and run themselves.

Operator begins a browser-agent preview

January 23

OpenAI introduced Operator as a research preview for ChatGPT Pro users in the United States. It could interact with websites through a browser, using actions such as clicking, typing and scrolling, while handing certain steps back to the user.24

The preview moved assistance from generating instructions toward carrying out actions, with the user kept in control of key steps.

DeepSeek concerns trigger a sharp Nvidia selloff

January 27

Nvidia lost roughly $590 billion in market value in a single trading day as DeepSeek’s models prompted investors to reassess expectations for AI spending, Reuters reported.27

The record one-day loss measured how much investors’ expectations rested on the cost of computing.

Janus-Pro combines image understanding and generation

January 27

DeepSeek released Janus-Pro, a multimodal model family combining visual understanding with image generation. Its design used separate visual encoding paths for the two tasks within a shared framework, and model weights were made available.28

The release extended the month’s downloadable-model activity beyond text and reasoning.

Qwen2.5-Max enters hosted competition

January 28

Alibaba’s Qwen team introduced Qwen2.5-Max, a large mixture-of-experts model offered through its hosted services. The team published comparisons with other models on selected benchmarks.29

The release broadened the choice of hosted services, offered through an API and chat interface rather than as downloadable weights.

Microsoft makes DeepSeek R1 available on its cloud

Reported January 30

Microsoft made DeepSeek R1 available through Azure AI Foundry and GitHub, saying it had run red-teaming and safety evaluations and applied content filtering by default.30

A US cloud provider began offering the Chinese model to business customers within days of the market’s reaction to it.

P-2025-W05-05

Ai2 scales its open post-training work

January 30

Ai2 released Tülu 3 405B, extending its post-training work to a 405-billion-parameter model. The release described training methods, including reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, and provided resources for reproducing and studying the work.31

The contribution included the training process as well as the resulting model, for researchers examining how post-training changes model behavior.

Italy restricts DeepSeek’s processing of personal data

January 30

Italy’s data protection authority ordered an immediate restriction on the processing of Italian users’ personal data by the companies providing DeepSeek’s service. It also opened an investigation after finding the companies’ response to its information request insufficient.32

The intervention brought European data-protection law to bear on the month’s most discussed model.

NASA blocks DeepSeek on agency systems

January 31

NASA’s chief AI officer told staff that DeepSeek was blocked on agency devices and networks, citing national-security and privacy concerns because its servers operated outside the United States. The US Navy had already told its members to avoid the model.33

Government restrictions on DeepSeek spread across agencies within a week of its rise to prominence.

OpenAI releases o3-mini

January 31

OpenAI released o3-mini in ChatGPT and its API, emphasizing mathematics, science and coding. The model offered different reasoning-effort settings, and ChatGPT’s free users received a way to try it.34

The release brought reasoning capability to a smaller model and a wider audience.

Deep research produces sourced reports

February 2

OpenAI introduced deep research in ChatGPT, initially for Pro users. The feature searched and analyzed material from the web to produce reports with citations.35

The service combined information gathering and synthesis in a longer workflow, with citations that made its sources easier to inspect.

February 2

The first provisions of the EU AI Act began to apply, including rules on prohibited AI practices and AI literacy. This was one stage in the law’s phased application.36

The milestone turned parts of the legislation into applicable requirements, with later stages on their own timetable.
