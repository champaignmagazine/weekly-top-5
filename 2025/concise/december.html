Introduction

December 2025

Major events

December opened with alarm at OpenAI. On December 1 Sam Altman declared a “code red” to improve ChatGPT after reports that Google’s Gemini 3 had overtaken it, delaying advertising and shopping agents to concentrate staff on the chatbot. Three days later Google brought Gemini 3 Deep Think to its top subscribers. Ten days after the code red, OpenAI released GPT-5.2, followed by GPT-5.2-Codex and a new ChatGPT Images model, and Google answered with Gemini 3 Flash across its products and a Deep Research agent developers could build on. DeepSeek released V3.2, Z.ai GLM-4.7, Nvidia its Nemotron 3 open models, and Runway both a new video model and interactive world models.

Governments moved on several fronts. On December 11 President Trump ordered the Justice Department to challenge state AI laws that conflicted with federal policy. Britain’s AI Security Institute published its first public report on frontier-model trends, China proposed rules for AI services that imitate human companionship, and the Energy Department signed up 24 organizations for the Genesis Mission. Deals marked the month: Disney licensed more than 200 characters to OpenAI’s Sora and agreed to invest $1 billion, Groq licensed its technology to Nvidia as its founder moved there, Manus joined Meta, and SoftBank completed its $30 billion investment in OpenAI.

Patterns and trends

Agents were built into the places people already work. Adobe put Photoshop and Acrobat inside ChatGPT, Accenture planned to train about 30,000 staff on Claude, Anthropic turned Agent Skills into an open standard, and xAI launched Grok for businesses. Nvidia spread well beyond chips, buying the developer of the Slurm scheduler, releasing open models and data, and licensing Groq’s inference designs. And the month ended on a warning: Geoffrey Hinton said AI had progressed faster than he expected and that he was more worried than when he left Google.

Long-term threads

December closed several of the year’s longest threads. The fight over state AI laws, open since attorneys general opposed a federal moratorium in May, became a federal order. SoftBank’s funding, agreed in March, was completed. Manus, which previewed its agent in March, joined Meta; the Genesis Mission, ordered in November, gained its first partners; Agent Skills, introduced in October, became a shared format. China’s draft rules took up the companion-chatbot concerns the FTC had examined in September. Disney’s Sora license extended the licensing market that had grown alongside the copyright cases since the Times–Amazon deal in May. Groq’s arrangement repeated the license-and-hire structure Google had used with Windsurf in July. And Hinton’s estimate that the tasks AI can handle double about every seven months echoed the measurement METR had published in March. The frontier, meanwhile, turned over faster than ever: GPT-5.2 arrived less than a month after GPT-5.1.

Week 49

December 1–7

Original post published December 7, 2025
P-2025-W49-01

DeepSeek releases V3.2 and a specialized reasoning variant

December 1

DeepSeek released V3.2 through its app, website and API, alongside the reasoning-focused V3.2-Speciale. The standard version combined reasoning with tool use. Speciale was initially offered through a temporary API endpoint and did not support tool calls.1

The pair separated two uses: a model that spends more computation on difficult reasoning, and one built to act through tools.

P-2025-W49-02

Apple reorganizes its AI leadership

December 1

Apple announced that John Giannandrea would step down as senior vice president for machine learning and AI strategy, serve as an adviser and retire in spring 2026. Amar Subramanya joined as vice president of AI, reporting to Craig Federighi, with responsibility for foundation models, machine-learning research, and AI safety and evaluation.2

Other parts of Giannandrea’s organization moved to Sabih Khan and Eddy Cue, placing Apple’s core AI work under its software leadership.

P-2025-W49-03

Runway introduces Gen-4.5 video generation

December 1

Runway introduced Gen-4.5, a video-generation model designed to follow detailed prompts and maintain motion and visual detail across a scene. Its demonstrations covered camera movement, complex compositions and interactions between objects.3

Runway also listed the model’s known failures, among them events shown in the wrong causal order and objects that disappear.

P-2025-W49-04

OpenAI declares a “code red” to improve ChatGPT

Reported December 1–2

Sam Altman told staff in a memo on December 1 that OpenAI was declaring a “code red” to improve ChatGPT, The Wall Street Journal and The Information reported. Priorities included speed, reliability, a wider range of answers and personalization; advertising, health and shopping agents and a personal assistant were delayed, and Altman encouraged temporary transfers to the ChatGPT effort.4

The memo followed reports that Google’s Gemini 3 had overtaken ChatGPT on industry benchmarks. OpenAI released GPT-5.2 10 days later.

P-2025-W49-05

Gemini 3 Deep Think reaches Ultra subscribers

December 4

Google began rolling out Gemini 3 Deep Think to Google AI Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app. The mode explored multiple hypotheses in parallel for difficult mathematics, science and logic problems. Google reported 41.0 percent on Humanity’s Last Exam without tools and 45.1 percent on ARC-AGI-2 with code execution.5

The mode had been announced with Gemini 3 in November and held back for safety testing before this release.

Meta acquires AI-wearables company Limitless

December 5

Meta acquired Limitless, the company behind a pendant that recorded and transcribed conversations. Financial terms were not disclosed. The transaction added a team working on wearable AI to Meta’s consumer-hardware efforts.6

The purchase extended Meta’s bet on AI built into everyday devices beyond its smart glasses.

Accenture and Anthropic plan a larger enterprise practice

December 9

Accenture and Anthropic announced a multi-year expansion of their partnership and a dedicated business group built around Claude. About 30,000 Accenture professionals were to receive training. The companies also planned tools to help clients measure value from AI in software engineering and develop applications for regulated industries.7

The agreement addressed the organizational work deployment requires: training, integration and measurement.

Adobe brings editing tools into ChatGPT

December 10

Adobe launched Photoshop, Express and Acrobat apps within ChatGPT. Users could request image changes, create designs and work with PDF documents through conversation, then use controls such as sliders to refine an edit.8

The integration made chat an entry point to established creative tools, combining spoken instructions with direct controls.

P-2025-W50-03

A 3D chip from a US foundry speeds AI workloads

December 10

Engineers from Stanford, Carnegie Mellon, the University of Pennsylvania and MIT, working with SkyWater Technology, reported a prototype monolithic 3D chip that stacks memory and computing layers linked by dense vertical wiring. Built entirely at SkyWater’s Minnesota foundry, it outperformed comparable flat chips by about four times in hardware tests; simulations of taller designs showed up to 12 times. The work was presented at IEDM.9

The team described it as the first such chip with clear performance gains made in a commercial foundry, showing the design could leave the laboratory.

P-2025-W50-04

GPT-5.2 expands the model family for professional work

December 11

OpenAI released GPT-5.2, with Instant, Thinking and Pro variants. Rollout in ChatGPT began with paid plans, while API access opened to developers. The company presented improvements in handling long documents, using tools, coding and producing work such as spreadsheets and presentations.10

The release, less than a month after GPT-5.1, was OpenAI’s answer to the competition that had prompted its “code red.”

P-2025-W50-05

A federal order targets conflicting state AI rules

December 11

President Donald Trump signed Executive Order 14365, directing the Justice Department to establish a task force to challenge state AI laws considered inconsistent with federal policy. It also called for a legislative recommendation for a national AI framework.11

The order took up the dispute over state AI laws that had run since May, when a proposed 10-year moratorium drew opposition from 40 state attorneys general.

P-2025-W50-06

Google opens Deep Research to developers

December 11

Google released a Gemini 3 Pro-based Deep Research agent through its new Interactions API. The agent planned searches, read results and searched again to fill gaps before producing a report, and developers could embed the process in their own applications. Google also released DeepSearchQA, a benchmark for multi-step web research.12

Research became a component developers could build on, not only a feature of a consumer app.

Runway announces interactive world models

December 11

Runway announced GWM-1, a family of models built on Gen-4.5 that generate video frame by frame in response to actions. Separate variants addressed explorable environments, conversational characters and robotic manipulation. Inputs could include camera movements, robot commands or audio.13

The release took video generation toward interactive simulation, a direction Google DeepMind had shown with Genie 3 in August.

Original post published December 21, 2025
P-2025-W51-01

Disney and OpenAI agree to bring Disney characters to Sora

Background: December 11; selected December 21; restored by the editor, October 2026

Disney and OpenAI announced a three-year agreement letting Sora users generate short videos with more than 200 characters from Disney, Marvel, Pixar and Star Wars; ChatGPT users could also create images of them. The license excluded the likenesses and voices of human performers. Disney also agreed to make a $1 billion equity investment in OpenAI. The agreement remained subject to definitive agreements and closing conditions.14,15

It was the first major licensing partnership for Sora, from a studio that had joined Universal in suing the image generator Midjourney in June.

P-2025-W51-02

Nvidia acquires Slurm developer SchedMD

December 15

Nvidia acquired SchedMD, the developer of the Slurm workload manager used to queue jobs and allocate computing resources on clusters. Nvidia said it would continue developing and distributing Slurm as open-source, vendor-neutral software.16

The deal put widely used open-source cluster-scheduling software under the leading AI chipmaker, with a pledge to keep it vendor-neutral.

P-2025-W51-03

Nvidia releases its Nemotron 3 open models

December 15

Nvidia introduced the Nemotron 3 family of open mixture-of-experts models in three sizes, from Nano, with about 30 billion parameters, to Ultra, with about 500 billion. Nano was available at once; Super and Ultra were expected in the first half of 2026. Nvidia also released three trillion tokens of training data and reinforcement-learning tools.17

Nvidia now supplied open models and training data for building agents as well as the chips that run them.

December 16

OpenAI began rolling out a new ChatGPT Images model and made it available through the API as GPT Image 1.5. The release focused on following editing instructions while preserving details such as composition, lighting and appearance, and introduced a dedicated Images experience in ChatGPT.18

Preserving what should stay unchanged reduces the need to regenerate a whole image for a small correction.

Groq announced a non-exclusive licensing agreement with Nvidia for its inference technology. Founder Jonathan Ross, president Sunny Madra and other team members were to join Nvidia. Groq said Simon Edwards would become chief executive and GroqCloud would continue operating.26

China consults on human-like AI interaction services

December 27

China’s Cyberspace Administration published draft measures for AI services that simulate human personality and communication in emotional interactions. The proposal addressed emotional manipulation, excessive dependence and harm to users’ mental health, with additional protections for minors.27

The consultation treated relationships between users and human-like systems as a regulatory concern, as US regulators had begun to do with companion chatbots in September.

NHS organizations use AI to forecast emergency demand

December 28

The UK government reported that 50 NHS organizations were using an AI tool to forecast demand in emergency departments. The system combined historical attendance with information such as weather and seasonal health data to support staffing and capacity planning, and was available to NHS trusts in England through the Federated Data Platform.28

The deployment applied forecasting to one of the health service’s hardest winter problems.

Hinton says AI has progressed faster than he expected

December 28

Geoffrey Hinton told CNN’s State of the Union that he was “probably more worried” about AI than when he left Google in 2023: “It’s progressed even faster than I thought.” He said AI had improved at reasoning and at deceiving people, and predicted it would gain the capability to replace many jobs in 2026.29

Hinton estimated that the length of tasks AI could handle was doubling about every seven months, the rate METR had reported in March.

Manus announced that it was joining Meta. The company said its general-purpose agent service would continue operating through its app and website, with subscriptions maintained and operations remaining in Singapore.30

P-2025-W53-04

OpenForecaster studies open-ended predictions

December 31 submission

Nikhil Chandak and colleagues trained language models on forecasting questions generated from news. Their OpenForesight dataset supported training of OpenForecaster 8B. The method used an offline news corpus to limit leakage of future information, followed by held-out testing on events from May to August 2025.33

The study measured accuracy, calibration and consistency in open-ended predictions about real events.

In the historical edition: 2025-W53-L3-1
P-2025-W53-05

AdaGReS selects context with less repetition

December 31 submission

Chao Peng and colleagues proposed AdaGReS, a method for selecting retrieved text within a fixed token budget. It balanced each passage’s relevance to a query against redundancy among the selected passages, using greedy selection and an adaptive calibration of that trade-off.34

The approach targeted a common weakness of retrieval-augmented generation: top-ranked passages that repeat the same information.

In the historical edition: 2025-W53-L3-2
P-2025-W53-06

RAIR tests relevance in product search

December 31 submission

Chenji Lu and colleagues introduced RAIR, a Chinese-language benchmark for relevance in e-commerce search. It combined general examples, difficult long-tail cases and a subset in which visual information mattered, and compared 14 open and closed models under a shared set of relevance rules.35

The benchmark tested whether a model could judge a product against a shopper’s request, including cases that depend on images.
