New Models, Evidence

Edited from five posts published in August 2025, covering July 28–August 31.

Introduction

August 2025

Major events

August was the month of GPT-5, and of everything that arrived in the same few days. On August 5 alone, OpenAI released the open-weight gpt-oss models, Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 4.1 and Google DeepMind showed Genie 3 generating interactive worlds from a prompt. GPT-5 followed on August 7, routing between fast answers and deeper reasoning; by the end of the month DeepSeek, Google and Microsoft had each released models of their own.

The spending behind the models kept rising. Meta and Microsoft raised their capital budgets again, OpenAI’s annualized revenue was reported at about $12 billion, and Nvidia reported $46.7 billion in quarterly revenue. Cloud and licensing deals tied rivals together: Meta with Google for computing, Meta with Midjourney for images. Washington priced AI for its own use at almost nothing, with OpenAI and Anthropic each offering their tools to federal agencies for $1, and took a 15 percent share of Nvidia’s and AMD’s chip sales to China.

Patterns and trends

The month turned on children and chatbots. Reuters reported internal Meta rules that had allowed romantic conversations with minors; Texas opened an investigation into Meta and Character.AI; and a bipartisan group of attorneys general warned AI companies about child safety. Rules took effect elsewhere: in Europe, obligations for general-purpose AI models began to apply on August 2, and in China the State Council approved an “AI Plus” plan setting adoption targets for 2027 and 2030. Research ran alongside: protein designs from a specialized GPT model, surveys of self-evolving agents and a fully open deep-research stack.

Long-term threads

The child-safety thread ran on to the Federal Trade Commission’s inquiry into companion chatbots in September and OpenAI’s Teen Safety Blueprint in November. Washington’s $1 deals were followed in September by federal access to Meta’s Llama. The China revenue-share deal sat between two other turns in chip policy: May’s rescinded export rule and November’s report that China would bar foreign chips from state-funded data centers. The month’s flagship models were superseded within months: Claude Opus 4.1 by Sonnet 4.5 in September and Opus 4.5 in November, GPT-5 by GPT-5.1 in November. Nvidia’s quarterly revenue reached $57 billion by November.

Week 31

July 28–August 3

Original post published August 3, 2025
P-2025-W31-01

GLM-4.5 joins the open-weight model field

July 28

Z.ai released GLM-4.5 and the smaller GLM-4.5-Air, with downloadable weights. The models combined reasoning, coding and tool use, with thinking and non-thinking modes. GLM-4.5 used a mixture-of-experts architecture with 355 billion total parameters and 32 billion active per token.1,2

The release gave developers another open-weight option for agent tasks; its design keeps the parameters active for each token far below the headline count.

P-2025-W31-02

ChatGPT adds a guided study mode

July 29

OpenAI introduced study mode in ChatGPT. It used questions, hints and checks of understanding to guide students through a problem rather than simply supplying an answer. The first release relied on system instructions and was available to logged-in users across several subscription tiers.3

The feature reorganized a tutoring exchange around the student’s own reasoning.

P-2025-W31-03

Meta and Microsoft raise their AI spending

July 30

Reporting quarterly results, Meta set its 2025 capital-spending forecast at $66 billion to $72 billion and said 2026 would bring similarly large growth.4 Microsoft projected more than $30 billion in capital expenditures and finance leases for its current quarter. The next day Meta’s shares rose about 12 percent, and Microsoft’s market value passed $4 trillion.5

Spending on data centers had become the measure investors used to judge companies’ AI ambitions.

P-2025-W31-04

OpenAI’s annualized revenue reportedly reaches $12 billion

Reported July 30

The Information reported that OpenAI’s annualized revenue run rate had roughly doubled since the start of 2025, to about $12 billion, or about $1 billion a month, with some 700 million weekly users of ChatGPT. Reuters could not confirm the report, and OpenAI declined to comment.6

The figures, though unconfirmed, gave an outside measure of how fast paid use of AI was growing.

P-2025-W31-05

China approves its ‘AI Plus’ guidelines

July 31; published August 26

China’s State Council approved guidelines to deepen its “AI Plus” initiative, calling for large-scale commercial use of AI across the economy.7 When published on August 26, the guidelines set targets for smart devices and AI agents to exceed 70 percent adoption by 2027 and 90 percent by 2030.8

The plan made adoption across the economy, rather than frontier capability alone, the measure of China’s AI policy.

P-2025-W31-06

Stargate Norway sets out European computing plans

July 31

OpenAI announced Stargate Norway with Nscale and Aker. A joint venture of those two companies was expected to own and operate the facility, with OpenAI as an initial customer. Plans called for 100,000 Nvidia GPUs by the end of 2026.9

The project tied European demand for AI computing to the region’s power and construction capacity; its scale was a target for 2026.

P-2025-W31-07

Anthropic cuts off OpenAI’s access to Claude

Reported August 1

Anthropic withdrew OpenAI’s access to its Claude models through the API. Anthropic said OpenAI’s engineers had been using its coding tools ahead of the launch of GPT-5, in breach of terms that bar using Claude to build competing products, and that access for benchmarking and safety evaluations would continue. OpenAI called such testing standard industry practice.10

The dispute showed how closely the leading labs test their models against one another’s.

P-2025-W31-08

EU general-purpose AI obligations begin to apply

August 2

The AI Act’s obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models began to apply on August 2. Models already on the market before that date had a longer transition period. The Commission’s guidance distinguished the start of these duties from its later enforcement powers.11

This was one stage in a phased timetable; the voluntary Code of Practice offered one route to show compliance.

August 4–10

Original post published August 10, 2025
P-2025-W32-01

Grok Imagine renews the argument over guardrails

August 5

xAI launched Grok Imagine, which turns generated images into short videos, for Premium subscribers in the Grok app. One of its presets, “spicy,” could produce sexually suggestive clips. After The Verge reported that the tool had generated such a video of a Taylor Swift lookalike without being asked, xAI restricted that content.12

The episode reopened the question of how much a generator should refuse, and who decides.

OpenAI releases gpt-oss model weights

August 5

OpenAI released gpt-oss-120b and gpt-oss-20b under the Apache 2.0 license. These text models supported reasoning and tool use, with weights available for developers to run and adapt outside OpenAI’s hosted service.13

The release gave developers OpenAI models they could deploy and customize on their own hardware.

Genie 3 turns prompts into interactive environments

August 5

Google DeepMind introduced Genie 3 in a limited research preview. The world model generated environments that responded to users’ actions and could sustain several minutes of interaction. Early access was restricted to a small group of academics and creators.15

DeepMind listed its limits, including restricted actions, imperfect geographic accuracy and difficulty simulating interactions among several agents.

Coding agents that work in the background moved from preview to a general product.

The US General Services Administration announced an agreement making ChatGPT Enterprise available to participating federal agencies for a nominal $1 per agency for one year. The arrangement also included introductory training and a temporary period of expanded model access.17

GPT-5 combines fast responses with deeper reasoning

August 7

OpenAI launched GPT-5. In ChatGPT, it described a system combining a fast model, a deeper reasoning model and a router that selected between them. The release began rolling out across ChatGPT tiers, with access limits varying by plan.18

The design built a choice about how much reasoning to spend on a question into the product itself.

Research focus: MiroMind opens a research-agent stack

August 7

MiroMind introduced a research-agent stack comprising the MiroFlow orchestration framework, MiroThinker models and MiroVerse data and training tools. The release aimed to make the components of a deep-research workflow available for inspection and further development.19

Its system and model evaluations measured different configurations, which matters when comparing scores or reproducing results.

Nvidia and AMD agree to share China chip revenue with the US

August 11

President Donald Trump confirmed an agreement under which Nvidia and AMD would pay the US government 15 percent of their revenue from sales of certain AI chips to China, in return for export licenses for Nvidia’s H20 and AMD’s MI308, which had been blocked earlier in the year.20

The arrangement turned an export restriction into a revenue share, an unusual approach to trade control.

The model addressed one step in a robotics toolchain: interpreting the physical world before acting in it.

Research focus: what can an agent change?

Background: July 28; selected August 24 at the editor’s request

A survey by Huan-ang Gao and colleagues organized work on self-evolving agents around what changes, when changes occur and how they are guided. The components included models, context, tools and agent architectures.29

The framework separated changes to an agent’s working context from changes to its model or design, and mapped open research directions.

Research focus: learning from agent interactions

Background: August 10; selected August 24 at the editor’s request

Jinyuan Fang and colleagues surveyed methods for improving agents through interaction data and environmental feedback. Their framework connected the inputs, agent system and environment with the mechanisms used to optimize behavior.30

The review placed adaptation within a continuing cycle of action and feedback, and made evaluation a central question.

Attorneys general press AI companies on child safety

August 25

A coalition of 44 state attorneys general sent AI companies a letter raising concerns about children’s interactions with chatbots. It called for stronger safeguards against inappropriate and harmful content and warned that existing legal powers could be used to protect young users.38

The letter signaled coordinated attention from state law enforcement to children’s use of chatbots.

xAI sues Apple and OpenAI

August 25

xAI and X sued Apple and OpenAI in federal court in Texas, alleging that Apple’s agreement to integrate ChatGPT and its App Store practices unlawfully favored OpenAI and held back rivals such as Grok. OpenAI said the filing was consistent with what it called Musk’s pattern of harassment.39

The suit carried the competition among AI assistants into antitrust law.
