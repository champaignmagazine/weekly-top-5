Agents, Reasoning

Edited from five posts published in March 2025, covering February 24–March 30.

Introduction

March 2025

Major events

March was the month the frontier models learned to think on request and agents began to act. Claude 3.7 Sonnet offered an extended-thinking mode with a thinking budget developers could set; OpenAI previewed GPT-4.5, built on scaled-up pre-training rather than added reasoning; Google introduced Gemini 2.5 Pro, built to reason before answering; and OpenAI’s native image generation in GPT-4o drew wide attention. Manus previewed an agent that could build a website or plan a trip, and Moderne and Salesforce moved agents into code maintenance and business workflows.

Capital and policy rounded out the month. OpenAI submitted its proposals for the US AI Action Plan, SoftBank agreed to buy the chip designer Ampere for $6.5 billion, Nvidia expanded its Cosmos tools for robotics, and Elon Musk folded X into xAI in an all-stock deal. The Computer History Museum, meanwhile, released the original 2012 source code of AlexNet.

Patterns and trends

Reasoning became something users and developers could switch on and budget, and measurement grew more demanding to keep up. METR estimated that the length of tasks agents could complete was doubling roughly every seven months; ARC-AGI-2 showed how far current systems fell short on abstract puzzles; and a working paper found ChatGPT less swayed than federal judges by legally irrelevant facts. The open-model world kept pace with the closed one: DeepSeek published a week of its infrastructure tools and updated V3, Alibaba opened Wan2.1 for video, Baidu released ERNIE 4.5 and the ERNIE X1 reasoning model, and Skywork extended reasoning to images.

Long-term threads

Reasoning on request spread through the year: o3 and Gemini 2.5 Flash’s adjustable thinking in April, Claude Opus 4 and Sonnet 4 in May, GPT-5’s router between fast and deep answers in August, and GPT-5.1’s adaptive reasoning in November. METR’s doubling estimate gave a yardstick for the agents that followed, from ChatGPT agent in July to coding agents built for long tasks in November. GPT-4o’s image generation reached developers in April, and OpenAI’s policy proposals fed the process that produced America’s AI Action Plan in July.

Week 9

February 24–March 2

Original post published March 3, 2025
P-2025-W09-01

Claude 3.7 Sonnet offers two modes of response

February 24

Anthropic released Claude 3.7 Sonnet with standard responses and an extended-thinking mode. API users could set a budget for the model’s thinking tokens. The release was available through Claude, Anthropic’s API, Amazon Bedrock and Google Cloud’s Vertex AI; extended thinking was excluded from the free Claude tier.1

The design made additional reasoning a choice within one model, with consequences for response time and cost.

DeepSeek shares its infrastructure tools

February 24–28

DeepSeek’s Open-Source Week published FlashMLA, DeepEP, DeepGEMM, parallelism tools including DualPipe and EPLB, and the 3FS file system. The releases addressed model decoding, communication, matrix operations, workload distribution and storage.2

Sharing these components exposed the engineering behind model training and inference that benchmarks alone do not describe.

Alibaba’s Wan team released inference code and model weights for Wan2.1, including text-to-video and image-to-video generation. The repository recorded the release on February 25.3

GPT-4.5 enters research preview

February 27

OpenAI released GPT-4.5 as a research preview for ChatGPT Pro users and developers. The company emphasized improvements from scaling pre-training and distinguished the model from systems that spend additional time reasoning before answering.5

The release showed that model development followed more than one approach: larger pre-training runs continued alongside explicit reasoning methods.

Moddy applies AI to changes across code repositories

March 4

Moderne announced Moddy, an agent for analyzing and changing code across multiple repositories. It combined a natural-language interface with the company’s code-analysis and transformation platform.7

The target was maintenance across an existing codebase, where the scope and consistency of changes matter more than generating new code.

Original post published March 16, 2025
P-2025-W11-01

Research shows DiLoCo training scaling predictably

March 12

Zachary Charles and colleagues reported scaling laws for DiLoCo, a training method that reduces how often data-parallel training must synchronize. When well tuned, it scaled predictably with model size and could outperform standard data-parallel training, with larger optimal batch sizes.10

The work supported training large models across less tightly connected hardware.

OpenAI submits proposals for US AI policy

March 13

OpenAI submitted recommendations for the US AI Action Plan, addressing regulation, export policy, infrastructure, energy, copyright and government adoption. The company presented the proposals as measures to support US leadership in AI.11

The submission showed how an AI developer sought to shape the conditions under which its industry would operate; the plan itself followed in July.

An experiment pits ChatGPT against federal judges

March 17

A Washington Post column by Jason Willick described a working paper by Eric Posner and Shivam Saran comparing federal judges with ChatGPT on the same case materials. The judges appeared more easily swayed by legally irrelevant factors than the AI.13

The experiment brought the question of AI in judicial decisions into a mainstream newspaper.

Cosmos expands its physical-AI tools

March 18

Nvidia announced additions to Cosmos for generating and interpreting data used in physical AI. Cosmos Predict and Cosmos Transfer were openly available, while the reasoning component was offered through early access.14

The tools addressed different parts of building robotics and autonomous systems: generating training material, adapting it and reasoning about physical scenes.

Skywork R1V extends reasoning to visual inputs

March 18

Skywork introduced R1V, a multimodal reasoning model with downloadable weights. The release combined visual inputs with step-by-step reasoning and was recorded in the project’s repository on March 18.15

The release extended the reasoning-model approach beyond text, with published evaluations on visual and mathematical tests.

METR measures the length of tasks agents can complete

March 19

METR measured agent performance against the time human experts needed for software and reasoning tasks. At a 50 percent success threshold, the estimated task horizon had doubled roughly every seven months across the historical models studied.16

The measure expressed task difficulty in human working time, giving agent progress a unit that could be tracked across models.

SoftBank agrees to acquire Ampere

March 19

SoftBank agreed to acquire Ampere Computing for $6.5 billion. The company described Ampere as a designer of energy-efficient computing chips based on the Arm platform. The transaction remained subject to closing conditions.17

The agreement expanded SoftBank’s planned investment in the hardware supporting AI.

AlexNet’s original source code becomes available

March 20

The Computer History Museum released AlexNet’s source code in partnership with Google. AlexNet was the image-recognition neural network developed in 2012 by Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever and Geoffrey Hinton.18

The release preserved for study how a landmark neural network was built.

DeepSeek updates V3

March 24–25

DeepSeek released V3-0324, an updated version of its V3 model, with downloadable weights under the MIT license. Its model card reported improvements across several evaluated capabilities, including reasoning and coding.20,21

The release continued development of a general-purpose model alongside the company’s R1 reasoning family.

ARC-AGI-2 raises the reasoning challenge

March 24

The ARC Prize team introduced ARC-AGI-2, a new collection of abstract reasoning tasks, alongside its 2025 competition. The launch report showed low scores for the evaluated AI systems and added explicit attention to the cost of solving tasks.22

The benchmark sought to expose gaps that earlier tests no longer captured as clearly.

GPT-4o adds native image generation

March 25

OpenAI introduced native image generation in GPT-4o, allowing images to be created and refined within a conversation. The announcement emphasized instruction following, text rendering and the use of uploaded images as context.24

The capability connected visual production to an ongoing dialogue, in which an image could be refined step by step.

Musk announces xAI’s acquisition of X

March 28

Elon Musk announced that xAI had acquired X in an all-stock transaction. He put X’s equity value at $33 billion, or $45 billion including $12 billion of debt; the figures were valuations stated for the deal, not a cash price.25,26
