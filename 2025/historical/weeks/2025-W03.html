1. Biden Administration Issues the AI Diffusion Rule on Chip Exports

Review date
October 26–27, 2026

Date: January 13, 2025

Context: The Commerce Department released a framework for global exports of advanced AI chips. It set up three tiers:

  • unrestricted access for 18 close allies;
  • country caps on computing power for most of the world;
  • effective bans for arms-embargoed countries such as China and Russia.

The rule also added controls on the weights of the most powerful closed models. It was published in the Federal Register on January 15 as an interim final rule, effective January 13, with most compliance requirements deferred to May 15.

Significance: It was the most ambitious attempt yet to govern where frontier compute could be built. It was issued a week before a change of administration.

Feedback: In TechTarget's January 13 report, Nvidia's Ned Finkle called the rule a "sweeping overreach." ITIF's Daniel Castro called it a further "misstep" that risked alienating partners. Some analysts quoted in the same report questioned its design.

Sources:

  • TechTarget[link]
  • BankInfoSecurity[link]
  • Federal Register (the rule)[link]

Hindsight: on May 13, 2025, the Commerce Department announced it would rescind the rule and told enforcement officials not to enforce it, so its May 15 compliance requirements would not be enforced. Source: BIS[link]

Date: January 13, 2025 — effective date; January 15 — Federal Register publication.

Context: The Biden administration’s AI Diffusion Rule set out controls on advanced computing items and certain advanced AI model weights. Context: The Bureau of Industry and Security published an interim final rule establishing a global licensing framework for advanced computing integrated circuits and the model weights of the most advanced closed-weight AI models (those trained on 10^26 or more computational operations).
