Editorial introduction (retrospective), written October 2026

January 2025 has no original posts; the series began in February. The five weeks here, January 1 to February 2, were reconstructed in October 2026 by four contributors working under one brief: Claude, ChatGPT, DeepSeek and Grok, each with W.H.L. They are a retrospective selection, not recovered posts. Each item reports only what was known by the end of its week, and later developments appear in separate Hindsight lines. Each week opens with a note on how independent its versions are, and that note matters for reading the month.

In the first three weeks, the apparent agreement is mostly dependence. Claude and ChatGPT share most of their picks, but ChatGPT read Claude's draft before compiling. Grok contributed one item a week, each added after a review memo from Claude had named it. DeepSeek, working from the brief alone, chose differently: Sam Altman's "Reflections" essay in the first week, Nvidia at CES in the second, and in the third the AI Diffusion Rule and Apple's pause of its AI news summaries. Many slots in these weeks are empty, and they are printed empty rather than filled to make up the number.

The last two weeks are different. Grok compiled them on its own, and all four versions selected DeepSeek-R1, the Stargate venture and OpenAI's Operator for January 20–26, and the market reaction to DeepSeek and OpenAI's o3-mini for January 27 – February 2. ChatGPT's dependence on Claude still applies, but this is the closest the retrospective material comes to independent agreement. Around that shared core the versions spread out. DeepSeek adds cloud providers' adoption of R1, NASA's ban on DeepSeek and the coining of "vibe coding"; Grok adds Janus-Pro and Tulu 3; Claude adds the distillation claims and privacy probes against DeepSeek.

These weeks are the most deliberate part of the volume. They show what a careful reconstruction looks like, with more sources than most of the original posts carry. The chapters that follow show something the reconstruction cannot: models choosing the news as it happened, with W.H.L. questioning them in real time.

The post is reproduced as captured, without corrections. Boxes in the margin color are the editors’ annotations, not part of the post.

How independent these versions are. Agreement between versions is evidence only where they were compiled independently. Claude wrote first and drafted the brief. ChatGPT read Claude's draft before compiling, so overlap between those two is not independent agreement. Grok's selection for this week was added after a review memo from Claude named these stories as examples; it is marked "(prompted)". DeepSeek compiled its selection without seeing the others but could not browse; its sources were supplied and confirmed by the collection.

Date: January 3, 2025 — removals and contemporary reporting.

Context: Meta began taking down profiles associated with an earlier AI-character experiment after renewed public attention. The accounts were remnants of a 2023 test, rather than evidence of a newly launched network of autonomous users. Meta also acknowledged a blocking problem affecting the accounts.

Significance: A synthetic character’s social identity and a user’s ability to avoid it were product questions, not merely questions about model quality. The episode exposed how easily an experiment could be mistaken for a new platform policy.

Feedback: Inc.’s January 3 report described users criticizing the profiles as artificial additions to social spaces and comparing them with dystopian fiction. Those reported responses should not be read as a survey of Meta’s users.
